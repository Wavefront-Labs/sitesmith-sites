History of cheesesteaks

Frizzled beef, cheese and bread were first brought together by Pat Olivieri, a hotdog stand owner in Philadelphia. In the early 1930s, tired of eating the same thing, Pat sent his younger brother Harry to the butcher for some steak at lunchtime.

Pat chopped the steak up on his grill and tossed it all on a roll. A local cab driver, a friend of Pat's, was curious about what Pat was eating and insisted on trying it. After one bite, the cab driver told Pat to ditch the hotdogs and sell this instead. Thus, the "steak sandwich" was born.

A few years later, cheese was added to the mix and voila! We have what we now call the "cheesesteak."

How we started

PJ first encountered the Philly cheesesteak while studying in the US. He then scoured the globe for the best place to bring this authentic flavour, from Europe to Asia to New Zealand. He settled on Australia because Aussies really love their meat and are renowned for it, so he knew they would appreciate it. Most of all, there was nothing like it in Australia.

In 2006, PJ came to Australia to do market research, and in 2008 he migrated from Korea. It was PJ's dream to bring American food to the people of Brisbane, and his nephew James helped with the finer details to pull it all together. After a couple of years benchmarking businesses all over the US, James moved to Australia in 2011 and joined the venture.

In late June 2012, the two finally opened the first PJ's Steaks shop in Brisbane.

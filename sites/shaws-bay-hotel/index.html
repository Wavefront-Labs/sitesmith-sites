The History of Fenwick House

In 1886 Captain Tom Fenwick, a strong-willed Scottish tug master, built a majestic two-storey mansion at the mouth of the Richmond River, overlooking Shaws Bay, so he could watch shipping movements from the upstairs verandah. Designed by his nephew James Fenwick, it was built with Scottish granite and a slate roof for approximately 9000 pounds, a large sum at the time.

Other features of this Victorian mansion include an English mahogany staircase, chandeliers, stained glass windows, four metre high ceilings and a magnificent Italian tiled entrance hall.

Captain Tom was an outstanding master mariner and an astute, ruthless businessman who came to dominate the steam tug business. Married twice, his second wife was Sarah Shaw. He was renowned for bravely rescuing ships stranded on the river bar, and died in 1896 from injuries and pulmonary disease after being submerged in seawater for a long period. He is buried in the small cemetery opposite the hotel.

Fenwick House was eventually sold by the family and has been a boys' boarding school, a hotel and a private residence.
